# Verdant Pay GRC Assessment — Methodology

## Overview

This document walks through the eight-stage methodology behind the Verdant Pay cyber risk assessment. Each stage was designed to build directly on the one before it, moving from defining what we were assessing all the way through to a prioritised action plan for reducing risk. Nothing in the final risk register was assumed or invented. Every finding traces back to evidence gathered during the assessment.

---

## Stage 1: Scope Definition

Before touching anything else, the first question to answer was: what exactly are we assessing and why? Skipping this step is one of the most common reasons risk assessments lose focus halfway through.

Four scope areas were defined: identity and access management, customer data handling, cybersecurity awareness and human risk, and governance and policy posture. Three areas were explicitly excluded with documented reasons. Scope boundaries were agreed before any information gathering began.

---

## Stage 2: Stakeholder Identification and Engagement

The right stakeholders are not always the obvious ones. Starting from the four scope areas, seven stakeholders were identified by following four logical trails: who owns each area, where sensitive data flows, who makes decisions, and who was closest to what had already gone wrong.

This produced a stakeholder list that included not just IT and compliance, but HR, the Cloud Administrator, the DPO, the CISO, and critically, the Senior Product Operations Manager, whose ground-level visibility into operational reality surfaced findings that no document or technical scan would have caught.

---

## Stage 3: Information Gathering and Current State Assessment

This was the most intensive stage of the assessment, conducted in four parts.

**Documentation review** established what the organization claimed its controls and processes to be, the formal paper trail of stated intent.

**Stakeholder interviews** went further, surfacing what was actually happening in practice. The gap between what documentation said and what interviews revealed was the most valuable finding source in the entire assessment.

**Technical discovery** verified the interview findings technically, checking account status, permission structures, audit logs, and configuration baselines against what stakeholders and documents had described.

**Control environment mapping** brought everything together, cataloguing every control across all four scope areas and rating each one honestly: strong, moderate, weak, or absent. Not whether the control existed, but how much protection it actually provided.

---

## Stage 4: Threat and Vulnerability Identification

With the control environment mapped, the next question was: given these gaps, what could actually go wrong? Nineteen risks were identified across the four scope areas, each with a specific threat, a specific vulnerability, a named asset at risk, and a realistic potential harm statement tied directly to Verdant Pay's regulatory and business context.

---

## Stage 5: Inherent Risk Assessment

Each of the nineteen risks was rated for likelihood and impact on a scale of one to five, producing an inherent risk score that reflects the raw danger of each risk before any controls are considered. All nineteen risks rated as critical at this stage, a finding that reflects both the severity of the identified control gaps and the high-stakes regulatory environment Verdant Pay operates in.

---

## Stage 6: Existing Control Assessment

Knowing a control exists is not the same as knowing it works. Each existing control was assessed specifically against the risk it was supposed to protect against, rated across five levels from high effectiveness to absent. No control across any of the nineteen risks reached high or moderate effectiveness, confirming that existing controls provided minimal protective value across the board.

---

## Stage 7: Residual Risk Assessment

Residual risk is what remains after existing controls are applied. By adjusting inherent risk scores based on actual control effectiveness, sixteen of the nineteen risks remained at critical level. The gap between inherent and residual risk was minimal, which is the honest result when controls are mostly weak or absent. This stage ensures the risk register reflects reality rather than the comfort of knowing controls are documented.

---

## Stage 8: Risk Register with Treatment Plans

The final stage turned findings into action. Each of the nineteen risks received a single accountable owner assigned using RACI principles, a three-layer treatment plan covering immediate actions within 30 days, short term actions within 90 days, and medium term actions within 180 days, a target risk score showing what the risk level should become after full treatment, and a priority ranking based on residual score, regulatory deadline, and whether fixing the risk would reduce other risks simultaneously.

Full implementation of all treatment plans would reduce Verdant Pay's risk profile from nineteen critical risks to zero critical risks.

---

## Key Principles Behind This Methodology

Every risk traces back to specific evidence. Nothing was assumed. Inherent and residual risk are kept separate to prevent weak controls from creating false assurance. Risk ownership follows RACI, one accountable person per risk. Treatment plans name specific people, specific actions, and specific deadlines. Vague recommendations do not get implemented.
