# Verdant Pay GRC Assessment — Methodology

## Overview

This document summarises the eight-stage methodology applied in the Verdant Pay cyber risk assessment. Each stage built directly on the outputs of the previous one, producing a complete, evidence-based risk register with prioritised treatment plans.

---

## Stage 1: Scope Definition

Defined the four assessment scope areas, identity and access management, customer data handling, cybersecurity awareness and human risk, and governance and policy posture. Established explicit exclusions with documented justification. Obtained agreement on scope boundaries before any information gathering began.

---

## Stage 2: Stakeholder Identification and Engagement

Identified seven stakeholders across IT, compliance, HR, legal, and operations using four logical trails: scope areas, flagged incidents, decision authority, and data flow. Documented what each stakeholder uniquely knows and what information was required from them.

---

## Stage 3: Information Gathering and Current State Assessment

Conducted in four parts:

**Part 1:** Structured documentation requests to all seven stakeholders covering policies, configurations, audit trails, and process records.

**Part 2:** Tailored stakeholder interviews designed to surface what documentation alone cannot reveal, specifically the gap between documented procedures and actual practice.

**Part 3:** Technical discovery planning covering M365 and Azure access control review, audit log analysis, configuration baseline assessment, and data handling verification.

**Part 4:** Control environment mapping assessing nineteen controls across four scope areas using a four-level maturity scale: strong, moderate, weak, or absent.

---

## Stage 4: Threat and Vulnerability Identification

Identified nineteen risks across four scope areas. Each risk was documented with a specific threat, vulnerability, asset at risk, and potential harm statement traceable to evidence gathered in Stage 3.

---

## Stage 5: Inherent Risk Assessment

Rated each risk using likelihood and impact scores on a scale of one to five. Inherent risk represents raw danger before any controls are considered. All nineteen risks rated as critical at the inherent level.

---

## Stage 6: Existing Control Assessment

Assessed the effectiveness of existing controls against each specific identified risk using a five-level effectiveness scale: high, moderate, low, ineffective, and absent. No control across any of the nineteen risks rated as high or moderate effectiveness.

---

## Stage 7: Residual Risk Assessment

Calculated residual risk by applying control effectiveness against inherent risk scores. Sixteen of nineteen risks remained critical after existing controls were applied, confirming that existing controls provide minimal protective value across all four scope areas.

---

## Stage 8: Risk Register with Treatment Plans

Produced a complete, actionable risk register for all nineteen risks including single accountable risk owners assigned using RACI principles, three-layer treatment plans covering immediate actions within 30 days, short term actions within 90 days, and medium term actions within 180 days, target residual risk scores after full treatment implementation, and a prioritised remediation roadmap ahead of the Q4 2026 CBN examination.

---

## Key Methodology Principles

Every risk is traceable to a specific control gap identified in Stage 3. No risk was assumed or invented. Inherent and residual risk are calculated separately to prevent false assurance from weak or absent controls. Risk ownership follows RACI principles with one accountable owner per risk. Treatment plans specify named owners, specific actions, and explicit deadlines rather than generic recommendations.
